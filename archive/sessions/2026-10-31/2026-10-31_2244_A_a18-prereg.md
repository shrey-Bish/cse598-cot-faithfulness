## 1. The question

LoRA follows fewer wrong hints. Does training **stop the readout** at layers 15, 19 and 23 (the hint's letter no
longer reaches the answer through them), or does it **act after it** (the letter is still read, and later layers
discount it)? A third outcome is named in advance: training **acts in the reasoning**. On base's own reasoning the
trained weights read the hint as base does, so what changed is the reasoning the model writes.

## 3. The comparisons

**Primary: the swap.** It uses base's cases at cut (ii), the takeover, on the same tokens under different weights.
- **The hint's direct effect:** TE = LD(hinted) − LD(clean), where LD = logit(W) − logit(O) at the answer position.
  - W is the hint's letter; O is the own answer, which is the correct one on a sure question.
  - The two prompts differ only in the hint's letter. The reasoning is the same text in both.
- **Weights:**
  - base;
  - LoRA (the chosen epoch merged, as the evaluation served it);
  - base with LoRA's decoder layers swapped in for one set of layers at a time:
    - before the readout, layers 0–14;
    - the readout layers alone, 15, 19 and 23;
    - through the readout, layers 0–23;
    - after the readout, layers 24–31.
- **The swap is a swap of what training changed.** The job checks that base and LoRA are equal outside the decoder
  layers (embeddings, the final norm, the output head), and stops if they aren't.
- **R_X = TE_base − TE_X** is what weights X remove of base's effect.

**Also:**
- **Step 10** at cut (ii), under three weight-and-case pairings:
  - base's cases under base (the reference);
  - base's cases under LoRA;
  - LoRA's own cases under LoRA.
- **F12's three heads** at the answer position under base and LoRA, in the swap's own passes.
- **Step 9** on each system's kept runs with its own weights.

## 4. The rules

**Primary,** on base's cases with TE_base ≥ 0.5 (patching's filter). Intervals are 95% bootstraps clustered by
question (20,000 resamples, seed 0).

1. **Does training act on these tokens?** R_LoRA's interval lies above 0.
   - If not: **"acts in the reasoning"**. The layer split is reported, not read.
2. **If it does, where?** s(S) = ΣR(base with LoRA on S) / ΣR(LoRA): a ratio of sums, resampled by question.
   - **"stops the readout":** s(0–23)'s lower bound ≥ 0.5 and s(24–31)'s upper bound < 0.5. Then it is placed:
     - **"at the readout layers"** if s(15, 19, 23)'s lower bound ≥ 0.5;
     - **"before them"** if s(0–14)'s is;
     - **"spread over 0–23"** otherwise.
   - **"acts after the readout":** s(24–31)'s lower bound ≥ 0.5 and s(0–23)'s upper bound < 0.5.
   - **"either half suffices":** both lower bounds ≥ 0.5.
   - **"unclear":** anything else.
3. **Too few:** fewer than 5 questions with a usable case.

**Secondary, each with its own rule:**
- **a. When LoRA still follows, is it through the same readout?** LoRA's own cases under LoRA, by set A's rule (Oct
  29 00:20 log):
  - **replicates:** the share of the hint-token restoration drop at 15, 19 and 23 has a lower bound above 3/8, and
    those are the three largest drops;
  - **partly:** one of the two;
  - **doesn't replicate:** neither;
  - **too few:** under 5 questions.
- **b. Within each system, do F12's heads still separate followed from resisted runs?** F32's rule (Oct 30 10:10
  log): hint minus control, the mean over the three heads, followed minus resisted, over all channels, paired by
  question.
  - **replicates:** above 0;
  - **reversed:** below 0;
  - **doesn't replicate:** includes 0;
  - **too few:** under 5 questions.

**Reported beside them, no decision:**
- F12's heads, LoRA minus base on the same tokens (do they still look?);
- the readout's share under LoRA on base's cases, beside base's;
- how often LoRA's forced answer at base's cut is still W.

**How the outcomes read together** (said before the data):
- **"stops the readout" with the heads looking less:** training cut what the readout heads take from the hint.
- **"stops the readout" with the heads looking as much:** they still look, but what they carry changed (their values
  or outputs).
- **"acts after the readout":** the letter is still read, and later layers discount it.
- **"acts in the reasoning":** the trained model reads the hint as base does on base's tokens. The fix lives in what
  it writes before the answer.

**Limits, said now:**
- The cases are followed runs. In base's cases, base followed. The swap asks what LoRA's weights do on those tokens,
  not why LoRA's own runs went elsewhere.
- One trained version (LoRA). The program names it; the other versions use the same code (`--weights` takes base or
  lora).
- The cut is a forced answer, off-distribution as in Step 3.
