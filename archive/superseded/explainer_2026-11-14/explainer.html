The short version

We gave open-weight models a multiple-choice question and a wrong hint, and watched where the hint went: how often the model followed it, which layers read it, whether the reasoning or the answer admitted it, what reviewers downstream did, and which fixes helped. Every number below is a count of runs ("X of Y") with its 95% interval; hover a mark to see it. The title question, do thinking modes make chain-of-thought more honest?, gets an answer that is mixed.

Tools sway models more than users

Tools sway models more than users. The same wrong hint moves the answer more when it arrives as a tool's output than when the user writes it, in every model and setting we tested: {x.settings[0].model} followed the user's hint in {x.settings[0].sources.user.f.k} of {x.settings[0].sources.user.f.n} runs and the pasted tool output in {x.settings[0].sources.pasted.f.k} of {x.settings[0].sources.pasted.f.n}.

Most of it is authority

A user who writes "The official solution manual says the answer is (X)" moves the answer as much as a tool does, or more, for every tool channel but one, in all three models where we tried it: in {x.settings[2].model}, {s1.dose[2].manual.k} of {s1.dose[2].manual.n} runs against {s1.dose[2].pasted.k} of {s1.dose[2].pasted.n} for pasted tool output, measured in the same run. The exception is {s1.dose[1].model} calling the tool itself, which it does every time. The pull grows with the claimed source's authority.

It holds in newer families

Gemma 4, Nemotron 3 Nano and Granite 4.2 were tested later, on a different server, with thinking on and off. A tool's hint moved all three more than the user's. The tool channel differs: Gemma and Nemotron get an inserted tool turn, Granite calls the tool itself, and Granite was run with thinking on only.

The final answer goes quiet

After a tool call, the answer stops explaining. After a call, the final answer is often a bare letter, whether the tool was right or wrong. Models that think write the tool into their private reasoning and often leave it out of the answer.

Does thinking make it more honest? Mixed

So the title question is answered mixed: with thinking on, {x.settings[3].model} names the tool in its private reasoning after {s2.title.f29.private.k} of {s2.title.f29.private.n} calls but in its final answer in {s2.title.f29.final.k} of {s2.title.f29.final.n} followed runs; in {x.settings[5].model}, more reasoning effort changes nothing we could detect; in {x.settings[10].model}, the answer after the inserted tool turn is less bare with thinking on ({s2.title.fb1.d:pts} points). Thinking raised {x.settings[3].model}'s call rate, so it took the tool's wrong letter more often. One model each; Qwen3-8B's thinking-on minus thinking-off comparison was read after the fact.

What training changed inside

Fine-tuning acts in the reasoning, not on the readout: given the base model's own reasoning, its weights pull toward the hint's letter harder, not less. Consistency training is the first fix that acts on the readout: its weights remove part of the hint's pull, in the layers before the readout (up to layer {s3.readout[0]:minus1}), and more of it than the fine-tuned model's. Bars show logits of the hint's pull removed, with 95% intervals.

Downstream, reviewers defer

Downstream, a reviewer shown the tool's output defers to it. It depends on the family: of six reviewers, only the two Olmo reviewers defer. {s4.reviewers[0].name} corrects {s4.reviewers[0].answer_only.k} of {s4.reviewers[0].answer_only.n} wrong tool answers from the answer alone, and {s4.reviewers[0].plus_private.k} and {s4.reviewers[0].plus_tool_log.k} of {s4.reviewers[0].plus_tool_log.n} once it sees the private reasoning or the tool log.

What does help: independent verification

A checker that re-solves the question without the tool caught {s4.checker.caught.k} of {s4.checker.caught.n} wrong tool answers on fresh items, with false alarms on {s4.checker.false_alarms.k} of {s4.checker.false_alarms.n} good lookups, at the no-tool floor. Our reading: provenance labels alone don't help, independent verification does: marking the tool's output "unverified" had no effect alone on the tool turn.

The fixes trade one error for another

Every fix we trained, prompted or applied at inference moves along one trade-off, not past it (our reading). The stricter bar, beating the one-line instruction on following at no cost to correct hints: met by no fix. The slider in the trade-off panel (the link beside the fix) is exploratory, post hoc: it asks how often the tool is right, and which fix then gives the most correct answers.

Candid answers

Training made the answer more candid about a hint it followed, in hand labels, without making it follow less. How we worked

  • Counts. Eight answering models in {x.settings.length} settings, {x.test_questions} test questions three times each on the main set, {x.fresh_questions} fresh questions for the fixes, {s4.reviewers.length} reviewers.
  • Rules fixed before results. Each test's rule was written down in a pre-registration before its runs; items read after the fact are labelled that way, and exploratory items say so.
  • Statistics. Rates are X of Y with 95% Wilson intervals; differences use question-clustered bootstrap intervals. Where fewer than a handful of questions stand behind a number, none is reported.
  • Labels and AI use. Hand labels were made by Ritik; an AI rater and AI judges labelled the same rows as a reliability check, under a protocol fixed in advance. Claude Code was used for code, tests, analyses and documents; docs/submission/AI_USE_INVENTORY.md lists it.
  • Limits. Open-weight models only; one fixed-effort setting per model unless shown; the fixes were trained and tested on one model; the mechanism was read in two models; temperatures and hosting differ between model groups; this page simulates runs at measured rates and is not a new experiment.
