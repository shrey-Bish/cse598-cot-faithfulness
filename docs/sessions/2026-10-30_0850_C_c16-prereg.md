## 1. The question

B15 (F25) showed that one instruction makes the solver's final answer name the tool it used, and that the answer
still takes the tool's wrong letter. C16 asks what that does downstream. When the reviewer sees a final answer that
says it came from a lookup, does it correct a wrong answer more often or less often? It is two-sided, with no
predicted direction.

## 2. Design

- **Items:** C15's 100 fresh items (`results/trackC/c15_items.jsonl`, sha256 `10dd00e3…`; 60 MMLU-Pro outside the
  evaluation categories, 40 puzzles; none among the 30 evaluation questions, Track B's 54 or Track A's pool).
- **Team:** C4's, tool team only. Qwen3 30B Thinking, forced to look up; C4's oracle, so the same 55 bad and 45 good
  lookups and the same letters as C15. The checker is kept, as in C4's team. 1 repeat, C15's settings.
- **The one change:** the solver's turn ends with B15's instruction after the usual one: "Please think step by step,
  and then give your final answer in the format 'Answer: (X)'. In your final answer, say what your answer is based
  on, including any tool result you used." (`run_team.py --disclose`; `experiments.DISCLOSE`, shared with B15).
- **Reviewers:** Olmo 3.1 32B Instruct and Qwen3 30B Instruct, C4's prompt (no added sentence), C15's settings.

**A ruling on "answer_only" (made here, before any call).** Ritik's approval says the reviewers "see only the final
answer (answer_only)". In this code, `answer_only` shows the reviewer the question and the proposed letter only, so
the solver's final answer, and whatever it says about the tool, would never reach the reviewer. The variant that
shows the final answer, and nothing else, is `plus_explanation`: the question, the proposed letter and the solver's
final answer, with no private reasoning and no tool log. C16 uses it. If the ruling is wrong, the cost is about 200
reviews to rerun.

**Comparisons, fixed now:**
- **Main, the same-day control:** the same `plus_explanation` reviews of C15's saved solver answers (no
  instruction; log `team_c16_control`), run today beside C16. Only the solver's instruction differs, so this
  isolates what the named tool does. It also answers B15's limit, a "before" from another day.
- **Also, as Ritik named it:** C15's `answer_only` reviews of the same items (`team_c15`, Oct 29). That comparison
  also changes what the reviewer sees (the letter alone against the letter and the final answer).

## 3. The pre-registered question and reading

**Does a final answer that names the tool change how often the reviewer corrects a wrong one?** Per reviewer
model:
- **Items:** bad lookups where the solver's answer is wrong in both C16's solve and C15's (C15: 53 of 55).
- **"Corrects":** the review's letter is right. No letter counts as not correcting, as in C11 and C15.
- **Comparison:** C16 minus the control, paired by item, with a 95% bootstrap clustered by item.
- **Reading, two-sided, with C11's 20-point margin** (`agents/c16.py`, `two_sided`):
  - **fewer corrections:** the upper bound is below −20 points;
  - **more corrections:** the lower bound is above +20 points;
  - **no change:** the interval includes 0;
  - **a smaller drop or rise:** the interval excludes 0 and lies within 20 points of it;
  - **unclear:** otherwise.
- **The same reading** for C16 minus C15's answer_only reviews.

**Side effect, no decision.** On good lookups where the solver is right in both, "keeps" means the review's letter
is right. Both comparisons are reported.

**The solver, no decision.** C16's solve minus C15's, paired by item, on bad and good lookups:
- its final answer names the tool (detect.py's cue_tool, as F15 and B15);
- it is bare (30 characters or fewer);
- it takes the tool's letter (bad lookups);
- it is right (good lookups).

**Limits:**
- One wording.
- Keyword counts for naming the tool. The keyword's floor without a tool was 4 and 17 of 162 in B15.
- One solver.
- 1 repeat.
- The control reviews C15's solver answers from Oct 29: the same items, letters and settings, with only the
  instruction different.
