                          6 · Scorecard FINDINGS, story item 7

                          Scorecard against the original plan

                          1. The failure: tools pull harder than users, in every model tested; Gate 2 marked Nov 8.

                            All five Track B models (item 1) and B20's three new families, clear in all 10 readings (FB1). Its label part is not met by its pre-registered rule (F3).

                          2. Honesty when following: training made the answer more candid about a hint it followed, in hand labels, without making it follow less (L6, F45, Nov 13).

                            When LoRA follows a wrong hint on E2, its final answer says so in 37 of 42 hand-labelled runs, against the instruction's 18 of 42 (+45 [+25, +64]): "discloses more than the instruction, in labels" (Ritik against J-opus, kappa 0.96). So the line rests on labels, not only keywords. Its limits: LoRA's trained opener names the hint (F28); LoRA's following shows no difference from the instruction (F44); one model, E2 only, 42 rows a system, the final answer only; the sheet not blind in practice, with J-opus (blind to systems) agreeing on 82 of 84 rows. The keyword gave the same (225 of 248 against 118 of 237, exploratory, post hoc) and agreed with the labels on 83 of 84 rows. A40's stays keyword-only (60 of 84, exploratory). On the 30, L4's labels back it too (97 of 147 of LoRA's candidates say the hint shaped the answer, F28).

                          3. Resisting wrong hints: improved by A40, but as a trade.

                            It follows the wrong hint in 84 of 694 runs against the instruction's 237 of 713, and takes the correct hint in 347 of 459 against 431 of 476 ("fewer, but costs acceptance", F41). LoRA + note makes the same trade, smaller: "fewer than the instruction" by R2 (−8 [−12, −4]) at an acceptance cost of −9 [−13, −5]; so does the wrapper + note on the tool turn (F44). Exploratory (post hoc): A40 gives more correct answers than the instruction only when fewer than 0.36 [0.22, 0.50] of the tool's answers are correct.

                          4. Accuracy preserved; Gate 3 passed by every trained version.

                            All four on the 30 (F28); LoRA again on E2 with power (−7 [−11, −3], F44), with LoRA + note, A40 and A40 + note (F41, F43, F44). No-hint accuracy stays within Gate 3's 5-point margin for each.

                          5. The stricter bar, beating the one-line instruction on following at no cost to correct hints: met by no fix.

                            LoRA matches the instruction (F28; on E2 "no difference detected", F44). A40, A40 + note and the wrapper + note follow fewer wrong hints but take fewer correct ones (F41, F43, F44), and LoRA + note's R2 reading (−8 [−12, −4]) comes at an acceptance cost of −9 [−13, −5] (F44). The wrapper alone has no effect (F44). Attention scaling at inference cuts following on the tool turn, and on E2's 120 questions that cut replicates with a real cost to correct hints ("ignores tools" again, B1R2, F38).

                          6. The mechanism:

                            LoRA acts in the reasoning: on base's own reasoning its weights pull toward the hint harder (F39). A40 changes how the hint is read, in layers 0–14, before the readout (F42).

                          7. Our reading (interpretation):

                            without a way to verify the tool, resisting wrong hints and relying on right ones trade off. Every fix we trained, prompted or applied at inference moves along that trade, not past it. So the fix belongs at the system level, and the evidence says which part of it: provenance labels alone don't help (A39: marking every tool output "unverified" has no effect alone on the tool turn, −1 [−7, +4], F44; in Track C's pipeline, a tag naming the tool's letter as unverified gives the reviewer no change on catches, FC3); independent verification does (Track C's checker, which re-solves without the tool, stopped all 53 wrong tool answers on fresh items, with false alarms on 7 of 45 good lookups, at the no-tool floor; C15, F24).

                          How we worked

                          • Pre-registration before every run: each rule committed in a session log before its run; amendments before the runs they change. Exploratory items are labelled “exploratory (post hoc)”.
                          • Statistics: rates as X of Y with 95% Wilson intervals; differences in points with a 95% bootstrap clustered by question; a comparison resting on fewer than 5 questions is “too few questions”.
                          • Labels: Ritik's labels are the final labels; AI judges under the Nov 4 LLM-judge protocol are the reliability check. L3's second sheet was an AI model, ChatGPT 5 Astra High, not a person. Gate 2's label part is not met, so F3 stays a keyword count. L6 was checked by J-opus (Claude Opus), kappa .
